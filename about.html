How We Score Stocks

Every stock is evaluated using a weighted model across four categories. Each category is scored 0–100 and combined into a final signal: 🟢 Buy (66–100) / 🟡 Wait (41–65) / 🔴 Avoid (0–40).

Growth — 35%

We weight growth as the largest factor because sustained earnings and revenue expansion is the strongest long-term driver of stock performance.

  • EPS Surprise (50%) — Did the company beat analyst earnings estimates? Positive surprises signal that management is executing better than expected and often trigger sustained re-ratings.
  • EPS Growth (30%) — Year-over-year earnings per share growth. Companies that consistently grow EPS compound investor returns over time.
  • Revenue Growth (20%) — Top-line growth reflects genuine business expansion, not just cost-cutting or financial engineering.

Valuation — 25%

A great business at the wrong price is a bad investment. We analyze valuation through three lenses that together reveal whether the market is pricing in realistic or unrealistic expectations.

  • PEG Ratio (50%) — Price/Earnings-to-Growth. A PEG below 1 suggests the stock is cheap relative to its growth rate. This is the single most useful valuation signal for growth companies.
  • FCF Yield (30%) — Free cash flow yield measures how much cash a company generates relative to its market cap. High FCF yield means the business is producing real cash, not just accounting profits.
  • P/E Ratio (20%) — The classic price-to-earnings ratio, benchmarked against sector averages to account for industry differences.

Quality — 20%

Quality filters out companies with weak fundamentals that may look attractive on price alone. We look for businesses with durable competitive advantages.

  • Operating Margin (35%) — High and stable operating margins indicate pricing power and operational efficiency — hallmarks of a durable business.
  • Insider Ownership (25%) — When executives and directors hold significant equity, their incentives are aligned with shareholders.
  • Return on Equity (25%) — ROE measures how efficiently a company generates profit from shareholders' capital. Consistently high ROE often indicates a competitive moat.
  • Current Ratio (15%) — A measure of short-term financial health. We flag companies that may struggle to meet near-term obligations.

Technical — 20%

Technical factors are secondary signals. We use them to assess entry timing and trend strength — not as standalone buy signals.

  • MA200 Position (40%) — Whether the stock is trading above its 200-day moving average. This is the most widely-followed trend indicator among institutional investors.
  • Distance from 52-Week High (25%) — Context for current price. A stock near its 52-week high in a healthy uptrend is very different from one that bounced after a 60% crash.
  • Short Float (20%) — High short interest can indicate bearish sentiment or set up a short squeeze. We use it as a risk signal.
  • RSI (15%) — Relative Strength Index identifies overbought (RSI > 70) and oversold (RSI < 30) conditions. Used to refine entry timing.

What We Don't Do

Bull Therapy does not predict the future or guarantee returns. Markets are unpredictable, and no model captures every risk. Our scores are a structured starting point for your own research — not a substitute for it. Always read the company's filings, understand the business, and consult a qualified financial advisor before investing.

What is Bull Therapy?
